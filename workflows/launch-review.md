# Can Marketing keep the launch date?

Ask your assistant, “Can Marketing keep the Thursday launch for Harbor? Check Engineering’s release plan and Marketing’s launch checklist. Show me what you’d tell the sponsor.”

Use the connections your assistant has for the relevant project records. Name the launch or link a work item if the assistant needs help finding it. With a missing connection, a link, file or pasted excerpt can fill the specific evidence gap.

1. **Reconcile the dates.** Find the approved launch date and Engineering’s current forecast. Keep the date, what it applies to, and its source together. Carry the source references into the next step.
2. **Trace the impact.** Check what Marketing needs and when it needs it. Compare that cutoff to when the input will be ready to use. Identify any confirmed alternative and any separate customer commitment. If the dependency or cutoff is unclear, give the PM the question that would resolve it.
3. **Draft the sponsor update.** State what can proceed, what is exposed and what decision is needed. Cite the records behind the recommendation. Leave the decision with the responsible person.

## Worked example

In this fictional project, the Harbor Jira ticket forecasts the production build for Friday. The approved Monday.com checklist sets the campaign for Thursday and requires a production build before publishing.

**Recommendation.** Escalate the Thursday campaign. The production build is forecast a day after the campaign needs it. Ask the launch owner to choose an approved alternative or authorize a new campaign date. The Thursday baseline stays in the update until the authorized owner changes it.

If the approved checklist instead says Marketing can use a staging build for this campaign and that build is already available, the campaign can proceed on the supplied evidence. Keep Friday’s production forecast visible because it could still affect a separate customer commitment.
