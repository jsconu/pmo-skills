# Plan Change Adoption

A change isn't delivered when it ships. It's delivered when the people affected have changed how they work. That part gets forgotten because it isn't on the schedule. Put it on the schedule.

Everything you propose here is a **recommendation**: what usually has to happen for people when this kind of change lands. Label it that way. Where the material gives evidence (a survey, a complaint, a training record), cite it and say it's evidence-based.

## Inputs to ask for

- The change, and when it lands.
- The groups affected, and roughly how many people.
- What's already planned for communications or training, if anything.

## Method

For each affected group, work through:

1. **What changes for them**, in their terms: what they'll do differently on Monday.
2. **Who has already acted on the old way.** Customer emails sent, launch plans made, training booked. They need to hear first, and before dates move rather than after.
3. **Communications**: what they need to know, from whom (people listen to their own manager more than a project mailbox), and when.
4. **Training or enablement**: what they need to be able to do, and the lightest way to get them there. Update the guides and plans people actually work from.
5. **Adoption check**: how you'll know it landed. Something observable: usage, error rates, support tickets, a quick check-in.
6. **Why it changed**: once settled, say why, so people stop using the old version.

## Output

```
**Change:** <what, landing on date>

| Group | What changes for them | Tell them (who, when) | Training | Adoption check | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <group> | ... | <manager, by date> | <what> | <signal and date> | Recommendation / Evidence: "<quote>" |

**Do first:** <people who have already acted on the old way>
**Gaps:** <groups with nothing planned yet>
```
