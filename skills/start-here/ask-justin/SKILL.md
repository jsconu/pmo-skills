# Ask Justin

You won't remember every skill, so ask. Describe where you are in the week and what's in front of you, and I'll point you to the right one.

Before saying what a skill does, read its SKILL.md. The summaries below are for orientation only.

Skills are organised by the **moment** you need help, not by PM knowledge area. Every skill works from what you paste in, so carry the output of one into the next.

<!-- map:start -->

## Frame the work

Starting something new: the sponsor, the charter, the project list, the backlog.

- **draft-the-charter**: Turn kickoff notes, a sponsor interview or a request email into a project charter that labels every field as confirmed, inferred or unconfirmed and lists who can close each gap.
- **find-the-projects**: Sort a messy batch of notes, emails, tickets or chat exports into the candidate projects and initiatives they describe, with aliases and the boundaries that need a PMO lead to confirm.
- **interview-the-sponsor**: Interview a sponsor or requester about a new project, one question at a time, until the problem, authority, scope and success criteria are clear enough to charter.
- **prioritize-the-backlog**: Put a backlog or list of candidate work into a reasoned order while keeping pinned items and fixed commitments exactly where they are, and show what moved and why.

## Run the week

The weekly rhythm of one project: meetings, status, risks and messages.

- **debrief-the-meeting**: Turn a meeting transcript or notes into a handoff that separates what was said, what was decided and what was accepted, with owners and dates left blank where nobody gave them.
- **draft-the-stakeholder-update**: Draft an escalation, customer update or stakeholder message from agreed facts, keeping every material risk, off-track status or open high-severity issue visible rather than softened.
- **triage-the-risks**: Read a task list, dependency export and current risk log and find the emerging exposures, each with its trigger, the work affected and a response worth considering (avoid, mitigate, transfer or accept).
- **write-the-weekly-update**: Write this week's project status update from last week's report and current evidence, covering what was finished, which milestones moved, which blockers persist and how long, and what decisions are needed.

## Test the status

Before anything goes to a sponsor: check the claims, the dates, the impact of a slip, and whether a change needs approval.

- **challenge-my-status**: Check a draft status report against the last approved report and the supporting evidence, and pick out the few claims a PM should challenge before sending, with both sources and replacement wording.
- **reconcile-the-dates**: Line up the dates in a schedule, a customer commitment and a delivery forecast, work out which refer to the same milestone, show the gap, and say what still needs approval.
- **review-a-change-request**: Review a proposed scope, backlog or schedule change against the baseline, identify which committed deliverables and milestones it touches, and route the decision to the right approver.
- **trace-the-impact**: Trace an upstream slip or change through its dependencies to find which downstream work is exposed, which can still proceed, and how much buffer is left.

## Run the portfolio

Across projects: steering, capacity, shared risks and where to spend today.

- **choose-todays-focus**: From current project briefs and pending decisions, pick the single most useful next action per project and order them for a leader's attention today.
- **find-capacity-conflicts**: Add up each person's allocated hours across projects and find who is over capacity, in which weeks and by how much, with a trade-off for the director to decide.
- **group-portfolio-risks**: Read risk registers from several projects and group similar exposures, with supporting examples, separating a genuinely shared cause from risks that only sound alike.
- **prep-the-portfolio-review**: Turn project briefs and the actions from the last review into a decision-first steering agenda, with the evidence behind each ask, the items the PM can handle without the committee, and a decision log to fill in at the close.

## Follow the decision

After the decision: did it work, did it land, who owns what, and are people ready.

- **check-the-decision**: Take a past decision, its expected result and fresh evidence, and say whether the intervention worked, what is still unknown and when to check again.
- **plan-change-adoption**: For a delivery change (a new system, process, date or scope change) and the groups it affects, propose the communications, training and adoption checks needed, labelled as recommendations.
- **plan-the-handoff**: Turn open reviews, follow-ups, owners and dates into a handoff that separates work that is assigned, work that is acknowledged by its owner and work that is actually done.
- **trace-a-decision**: Take an approved change and the team artifacts that should reflect it (plans, tickets, schedules, customer commitments, messages) and show where the decision appears, where the evidence still shows the old position, and who should verify adoption.

## Measure and learn

Money, metrics and lessons.

- **check-the-success-metrics**: Compare success metric definitions, targets and direction with the latest observations, showing movement against each target, missing measures and questions to ask.
- **find-recurring-lessons**: Compare decisions and their outcomes across comparable projects to find candidate lessons, the counterexamples, and the limits on applying them elsewhere.
- **review-the-budget**: From a budget, logged hours, rate assumptions and expenses, calculate actual spend, variance against budget and any unexplained gaps, with every figure traceable.
- **run-the-retrospective**: From a project's charter, decision history, delivery evidence and closeout notes, write a retrospective covering what happened, which interventions helped and what to repeat, with lessons specific enough for the next PM to use.

## Common chains

- **New project:** interview-the-sponsor, then draft-the-charter.
- **A slip turns up in a meeting:** debrief-the-meeting, then trace-the-impact, then challenge-my-status on the report that has to mention it.
- **Steering:** prep-the-portfolio-review before, fill its decision log at the close, then check-the-decision at the next review and trace-a-decision to see whether it landed.
- **Closing out:** run-the-retrospective per project, then find-recurring-lessons across several.

## Nothing fits

Say what you were trying to do. If no skill covers it, answer directly and suggest a "skill idea" issue at https://github.com/jsconu/pmo-skills/issues with a fictional example of the situation.
