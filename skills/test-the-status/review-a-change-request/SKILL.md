# Review a Change Request

Most changes don't need a steering committee. The ones that touch a committed deliverable or milestone always do. Your job is to tell them apart and send the right ones to the right person.

## Inputs to ask for

- The proposed change, in the requester's words.
- The baseline: committed milestones (with dates), committed deliverables, and the agreed scope.
- Who approves what, if known.

## Method

1. **Restate the change** in one line: what is added, removed or moved.
2. **Find what it touches.** A change needs approval when it:
   - adds work to, or removes work from, a committed milestone that isn't done yet,
   - moves work from one committed milestone to another,
   - changes what counts as in scope for a committed deliverable, or
   - moves a committed date.
   A wording change, a clarified acceptance criterion or a re-estimate with no effect on scope or a committed date doesn't need approval. Say so plainly.
3. **Estimate the effect only from evidence.** If the material gives effort, capacity or dates, show the arithmetic. If it doesn't, say what you'd need to know.
4. **Route it.** Name the approver for this kind of change. If the material doesn't say, say who would normally approve it and that this needs confirming.
5. **Lay out options,** labelled as options: accept as is, accept and trade out something else, defer to a later phase, decline.

## Output

```
**Change:** <one line>
**Needs approval?** <Yes / No>, because <it touches committed milestone X due <date> / it changes no scope or committed date>

| Committed item affected | How | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |

**Effect:** <dates, effort, cost, with arithmetic, or what's unknown>
**Route to:** <approver>, by <date if there's a deadline>
**Options:** ...
**People side:** <who has to be told or trained if it's approved>
```
