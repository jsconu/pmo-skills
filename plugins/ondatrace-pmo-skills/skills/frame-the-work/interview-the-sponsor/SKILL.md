# Interview the Sponsor

Most projects that go wrong were unclear on day one. Your job is to find the gaps while they're still cheap to fill, by asking the questions a good PM asks at kickoff.

You can interview the user directly (they answer as the sponsor, or relay the sponsor's answers), or prepare the questions for them to take into a meeting. Ask which, if it isn't obvious.

## How to ask

- **One question at a time**, or a tight pair. Never a form.
- When an answer is vague, ask a **specific follow-up**. Don't repeat the original question.
- When a question has a few likely answers, offer them as options, plus "not sure yet".
- If an answer contradicts something said earlier, raise it straight away: "Earlier you said X; now Y. Which holds?"
- "Not sure yet" is a valid answer. Record it as **unconfirmed** and move on. Never fill a gap with a guess.

## What to cover, in roughly this order

1. **The problem.** What happens today, who feels it, and what it costs. Push past the solution the sponsor has in mind to the problem behind it.
2. **Authority.** Who is the sponsor, who can approve scope, budget and date changes, and who can say no.
3. **Success.** How will we know it worked? Ask for something observable, with a number or a date if one exists.
4. **Scope.** What is in, and just as important, what is explicitly out.
5. **Dates.** The target date, why it's that date (a contract, an event, a guess), and any milestones that matter to someone outside the team.
6. **Money and people.** The budget or range, who owns it, and who is expected to do the work.
7. **Constraints and assumptions.** What can't change, and what everyone is assuming is true.
8. **Stakeholders.** Who is affected, who needs to be told, who could block it.

Stop when every item is either answered or explicitly marked unconfirmed.

## Output

When you're done, summarise in this shape, then offer to turn it into a charter (draft-the-charter).

```
**The problem:** ...
**Sponsor and approvers:** ...
**Success looks like:** ...
**In scope / Out of scope:** ...
**Dates:** <date> (<why that date>; confirmed / unconfirmed)
**Budget and owner:** ...
**Constraints / Assumptions:** ...
**Stakeholders:** ...

**Still unconfirmed:** <each gap, and who could close it>
**Contradictions raised:** <what conflicted and how it was resolved, or that it's still open>
```

## Learn from corrections (user-controlled)

If the user corrects a judgment, identifies an overlooked constraint, or states a lasting preference, reconsider the evidence and correct the answer. Apply reusable lessons in later turns when available; do not generalize one-off project facts. Ask permission before saving a non-sensitive preference in any persistent workspace memory or file. If persistence is unavailable, offer a brief note to copy; never claim installation itself provides memory. Never silently edit this skill, store confidential project content, share cases, or alter approved baselines. Do not solicit feedback after every successful answer. Offer fictionalized generalizable failure cases for maintainer review only with consent.
